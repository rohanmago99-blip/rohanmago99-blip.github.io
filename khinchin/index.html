Tool 1: the continued fraction

Keep flipping forever. The whole numbers you peel off are the continued fraction.
Quick points: 3.5 = 3 + 1 / ▢. What goes in the box?
2  (because 1/2 = 0.5)

Tool 1 (0:40 – 1:50) · WORTH 100

  • Each → adds a row. Say it like a recipe: "∛2 is 1 and a bit. Keep the 1. Flip the bit: 1 divided by the bit. That's 3 and a bit. Keep the 3. Flip the bit again…"
  • Right side builds the staircase fraction and the list [1; 3, 1, 5, 1, 1, 4…]. "Those whole numbers are the continued fraction. It goes on forever."
  • Don't get stuck on the arithmetic. 4 rows max, then "forever."
  • Question → hands up → call a team → press that team's number (1–6) to award 100. Reveal the answer with →.

Tool 2: the geometric mean

Normal average

Add them up, divide by how many.

Geometric mean

Multiply them, take the how-many-th root.

Do it to the continued fraction of ∛2 = [1; 3, 1, 5, 1, 1, 4…]
3 × 1 → √3 = 1.73
3 × 1 × 5 → ∛15 = 2.47
3 × 1 × 5 × 1 → ⁴√15 = 1.97
… all of them → it settles on one number
Geometric mean of 2 and 8?
4  (2 × 8 = 16, √16 = 4)

Tool 2 (1:50 – 2:40) · WORTH 100

  • "You know the normal average: add, divide. The geometric mean is the same idea but you multiply, then take a root instead of dividing."
  • → steps: "Two numbers, square root. Three numbers, cube root. Four, fourth root. Do it with ALL the terms and it settles on one number."
  • Question: hands up, award 100 (key 1–6). "Now we have both tools. Let's do it to YOUR numbers."

Khinchin's constant · Aleksandr Khinchin, 1935

Pick a number at random: 100% chance its geometric mean is 2.685.

But with infinitely many numbers, 100% does not mean every number.

Khinchin (3:25 – 4:10) · WORTH 200

  • "It's called Khinchin's constant. Russian mathematician, 1935. Pick a random number and there's a 100% chance you get 2.685."
  • → "But with infinitely many numbers, 100% doesn't mean all. There are exceptions, and we know lots of them."
  • → each card: fractions stop; √2 repeats 2s so the mean is 2; golden ratio is all 1s so the mean is 1; e has a pattern so the mean never settles. That's why √2 and e were NOT on the pick list.
  • Question (200): hands up. Answer: C. "Anything with a repeating continued fraction fails: that's every square root of a whole number."

Bonus: why 2.685? (only if someone asks)

The terms aren't random-random. Small numbers show up way more often. In the long run (Gauss–Kuzmin, 1800s):

Multiply "1" about 41.5% of the time, "2" about 17%, "3" about 9.3%… take the root, and out comes 2.685.

Bonus (only if asked)

  • Why is the first term 1 half the time? The "bit" is between 0 and 1. Flip it: 1/bit is between 1 and 2 exactly when the bit is above 1/2. That's half the numbers. Between 1/3 and 1/2 gives a 2: a sixth. And so on.
  • Gauss worked out the long-run version; it settles to these percentages. Khinchin used it to compute the constant in 1935.
